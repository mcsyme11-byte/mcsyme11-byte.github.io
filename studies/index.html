Winglets and induced drag

Research paper · October 28, 2026

Why do most airliners have upturned wingtips? The paper explains the physics of induced drag, follows NASA's research of the 1970s into service, and compares the designs flying today.

Every figure comes from NASA research, manufacturer and airline publications or industry sources, thirteen references in all. It also covers what winglets cost: extra weight, a higher bending load at the wing root, and airport gate limits on wingspan.

The second half applies the research. I designed a 1:35 scale display model in CAD: one wing section and seven wingtips that swap by hand, each sized from the published figures and made to fit a 180 mm print bed.

The equation at the centre of it

CDi = CL² ÷ (π × AR × e)

Induced drag falls when the aspect ratio AR or the span efficiency e rises. Winglets work on both.

Worked example, A320-sized wing: raising e from 0.80 to 0.88 cuts induced drag by about 9%.

Alpha I flight mathematics

Working paper · June 2026

Before any part of my rocket is built, its flight is worked out on paper: the air at the launch site, the motor, the speed off the rail, the rocket equation, the load on the airframe and the size of each parachute.

It is a first revision, and a second is under way. The worked calculations are laid out as cards on the Project Alpha page.
