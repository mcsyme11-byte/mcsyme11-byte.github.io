The short version

How I got here

I've been earning my own money since 2019, starting with snow, yards and construction help for neighbours. In 2023 that turned into Syme Skyworks, my own drone services and construction support company, where I fly inspections and surveys, run the jobs and send the invoices.

In 2026 I started building software. In a few months that became the Flight Deck Suite (seven connected apps for flight simulation, with a public release), Hot Mic (private voice dictation that runs entirely on your own computer) and NASA and ESA Data Projects (nine live tools built on real space data). Along the way I launched Simply Aerospace, where I explain aerospace at three levels, from no math to real equations.

I build with AI, and I think that's worth being clear about: Claude writes most of the code, and my job is everything around it. I decide what's worth building, write the briefs, approve the mock-ups, test on real flights, and say no when something isn't good enough to ship.

How I work

Rules I actually follow
01

Plan first, build on "go"

Questions get answered before anything changes. Nothing is built, installed or published until I say go.

02

Mock-ups before pixels

Every new page starts as a mock-up I approve. If it disappoints, the plan changes, not my standards.

03

Nothing untested near the simulator

After one bad day in October 2026, every request an app sends to the sim is tried safely by itself first.

04

Every fact from a named source

Runways from the current chart. Data from the agency's own documentation. Anything unconfirmed is marked, never guessed.

05

Write it down

Each project keeps a roadmap, a change log, a history and a project record that rebuilds itself after every change.

06

Measure, don't guess

Smoothness is measured in frames per second, tests are counted, and a release isn't done until the checklist is.
